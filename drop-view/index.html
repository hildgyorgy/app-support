Drop & View

A browser-based 3D model viewer

About

Drop & View is a small, browser-based viewer for inspecting 3D models without installing a desktop application. Drop a model onto the start screen, or choose it with the file picker, and explore it interactively.

System requirements

Drop & View runs in a modern browser with JavaScript and WebGL support. No account or server-side upload is required.

How to use

  1. Drag a .glb, .fbx or .gltf file onto the page, or choose OPEN FILE in the top-left corner. Choose DEMO on the start screen to try the viewer without your own file.
  2. Drag with the left mouse button to orbit around the model. Scroll or pinch to zoom; drag with the right mouse button to pan.
  3. Double-click a point on the model to bring it smoothly to the centre of the view. In ORTHO mode, this keeps the current viewing direction and zoom.
  4. Use the display styles at the top centre and the camera modes at the top right to explore the model.
  5. Choose ORBIT for the standard model view, or FLY on a desktop computer for free flight through the model. FLY is disabled on touch-only devices.
  6. Open CONTROLS at the bottom left for lighting, transparency, camera FOV and FLY speed, or SECTION at the bottom right for section settings.

Finding your way around

The Controls, Ortho and Section menus start closed to leave more room for the model. Click their labels to open or close them. Click on the model area or press Escape to close the menus. On small screens, opening one menu closes the others.

Red labels and black markers identify selected views. The red circle with an i beside OPEN FILE opens this support page in a new tab.

Choosing a model format

The export format makes a noticeable difference to the result. For the best visual quality in Drop & View, start with GLB whenever possible.

GLB — recommended for visual quality

Advantages: in our testing, GLB gives the most faithful overall transfer and the most consistent materials and textures. It can package the geometry and textures into one file, making it convenient to send to a client.

Trade-off: conversion quality still depends on the source model and exporter. Textures or material settings may need adjustment, and converting a file cannot restore textures that were missing from the export.

Drop & View GLB Exporter for Archicad

For a direct Archicad workflow, the Drop & View GLB Exporter creates a self-contained GLB from the active 3D window. It exports visible geometry, surface colours, transparency, embedded image textures, smooth shading and graphical overrides, and can preserve surface-based mesh groups for visibility controls. The current beta supports Archicad 28 and 29 on Apple Silicon macOS 26 or later and Windows 11.

Install the package from GitHub Releases, add it through Archicad's Add-On Manager, then choose Drop & View GLB Exporter → Export active 3D window to GLB… from an active 3D window.

FBX — a quick route from Archicad

Advantages: native FBX export offers a quick way to try a model in the viewer, usually with accurate geometry. Graphisoft introduced native FBX import and export in Archicad 27, so availability depends on your Archicad version.

Limitations: FBX results can differ between Archicad versions and export workflows. A model may arrive with a different axis orientation, while materials and textures can look darker or otherwise different from the original. Some exported files may not load correctly. FBX is useful for a quick result, but it is less consistent than GLB in our testing.

If your FBX looks wrong or does not open, try importing it into Blender, checking its orientation and materials, and exporting a GLB. An online converter is another option, but check the converted model before sharing it.

GLTF and textures

GLTF is also supported, but some files refer to separate texture images or binary data. The viewer opens one selected file at a time, so models that depend on local companion files may not load correctly. A self-contained GLB with embedded textures is the simplest choice.

Display styles

  • ORIGINAL: displays the imported materials, colours and textures under the viewer's lighting.
  • WHITE: shows a neutral white model while keeping recognised glass transparent.
  • HIDDEN: combines white surfaces with visible edge lines.
  • WIRE: shows the model's wireframe. Shadows and sun controls are disabled in this mode.
  • B&W: creates a graphic black-and-white view from the sunlight and shadows.

Camera views

PERSP gives a perspective view. AXON gives an axonometric view with parallel projection. Both allow orbiting around the model.

ORTHO opens the TOP, FRONT, LEFT, RIGHT and BACK presets. The four elevation views can be rotated horizontally with the left mouse button: the building stays upright and the projection remains orthogonal. This lets you inspect angled or curved facades without switching to perspective. TOP remains a fixed overhead view.

When you rotate away from an exact elevation, its preset highlight clears while ORTHO stays selected. Click an elevation name to return to that exact view. Panning, zooming and double-click centring remain available.

Light and viewing controls

Open CONTROLS to adjust SUN DIRECTION and SUN HEIGHT, toggle SHADOW, ENV LIGHT and AMBIENT, or change CAMERA FOV. Environment light and ambient occlusion are available in ORIGINAL mode; FOV is available in PERSP mode and disabled in AXON and ORTHO. The panel also includes the FLY speed control; holding SHIFT while flying temporarily doubles the speed.

TRANSPARENCY changes the alpha of transparent materials. Move the slider left for clearer materials or right for more opaque materials. It is available in ORIGINAL, WHITE and HIDDEN modes and disabled in WIRE and B&W, where the viewer uses fixed display treatment. The slider affects transparent materials identified by the imported file; it is not limited to materials named “glass”.

Sections and inspection

Click the small circle beside SECTION to switch cutting on or off. A red circle means the section is active. Click the SECTION label to open the axis menu, choose X, Y or Z, and move the slider to reveal the inside of the model.

Closing the axis menu does not switch off the section. While cutting is active, the position slider stays available.

FLY navigation

On a desktop computer, click FLY to enter free flight. Move the mouse to look around and use WASD or the arrow keys to move; E and Q move vertically. Hold SHIFT for a temporary 2× speed boost.

A single click on the model pauses FLY and releases the pointer without switching back to ORBIT. Click the model again to resume. While paused, you can use the viewer controls and choose ORBIT. Press ESC at any time as a fallback to release the pointer.

If a file does not open

The viewer returns to the start screen and shows a red error message in place of the privacy note. You can choose another file, drop a new model onto the page or try the demo. The usual privacy note returns when you start another loading attempt.

Check the file format and whether the model includes its textures. For inconsistent FBX results, try the GLB workflow above. Large or complex models may take longer to open and can be demanding on phones or older computers.

Sharing with clients

Send the model file together with the viewer's web address. The recipient opens the website and drops the file onto it. No account or login is needed. There is currently no model-upload service or shareable link that stores your model online.

Privacy

Drop & View processes the selected model in your browser. The app does not require an account and does not upload your model to a developer-operated server.

The viewer loads Three.js and, when needed, its Draco decoder from jsDelivr. Choosing DEMO downloads the sample model from the app's website.

If you choose to use an external online converter, that is a separate service: its upload process and privacy terms are independent of Drop & View. Blender conversion can be performed locally on your computer.
